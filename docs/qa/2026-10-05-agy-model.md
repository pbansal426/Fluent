## 3. Top 5 Things That Would Stop a Grandma

1. **Deadlock on Combined City/State/ZIP Code Fields (W-4 Blocker)**  
   When the assistant asks *"What is your city, state, and ZIP code?"*, a grandmother naturally answers with her full city and zip (e.g., *"Dallas, TX 75201"*). Because the backend code sees the phrase *"código postal"* in the field label, it enforces a strict postal code validator (`isZip`) that rejects the answer with *"That does not look like a postal code. Please say it again."* Even typing just the 5-digit zip code fails. A senior cannot proceed through this question without knowing to type the magic keyword *"skip"*.

2. **Failing to Recognize "I don't have one" or "No other names" (Form I-9 & I-765)**  
   Older and immigrant users frequently do not have middle names or aliases. When asked for a middle initial or alias and replying *"I don't have one"* or *"No other names"*, the assistant rejects their response as an invalid name error (*"Sorry, I did not understand. Can you say it a different way?"*). Squeezing an elderly person into an error loop when they gave an honest, accurate negative answer will cause them to abandon the form.

3. **Inverted PDF Field Order Corrupting Applicant Legal Names (Form I-765)**  
   On USCIS Form I-765, the internal PDF structure lists "Other Names Used" (aliases) before "Your Full Legal Name". The assistant asks for aliases first. When the user says *"My legal name is Elena Gomez"*, the assistant blindly dumps *"Elena"* into the alias box (`Line2b`) and discards the surname *"Gomez"*. An elderly applicant would unknowingly submit an official USCIS immigration form with their real name entered as an alias and their primary legal name completely blank.

4. **Cryptic Opening Questions and Hallucinated Explanations (Form I-765)**  
   The very first thing an applicant hears on Form I-765 is: *"Is this part needed? If a question is not for you, just say skip."* An older person has no context for what "this part" refers to. When they ask *"what is this part?"*, the assistant hallucinates that it is *"for you to write anything extra"*, when in reality it is the Attorney/Representative (Form G-28) declaration. This directly misleads vulnerable seniors about legal representation requirements.

5. **Leaked Developer Code, Raw PDF Tooltips, and Bizarre "Caution" Questions**  
   Low-literacy users need warm, plain 6th-grade language. Instead, they are hit with:
   - Tooltip fragments: *"only one box): 1.A Select Initial Permission to accept employment..."* ending in clipped dots (`….`).
   - Hallucinated section header prompts: *"Do you have any cautions to add?"* when attempting to ask about tax filing status on Form W-4.
   - Persistent banner warnings: *"Tap the microphone to answer by voice, or type your answers below."* when audio input is disabled.

## 4. Section 5 Audit: Grandma Usability & Cognitive Load Analysis

- **Readability at a glance**:  
  The two-pane split (form iframe left, sidebar right) provides good visibility into the form. However, text inside the PDF viewer defaults to 100% zoom, making multi-column USCIS forms tiny and hard to read for seniors with visual impairments.
- **Contrast & Text Size**:  
  Sidebar typography uses clean system fonts with acceptable contrast (dark slate on near-black background). The blue action buttons and green checkmark bubbles (`✓ Legal name...`) have good contrast. However, the placeholder text (`Type your answer…` / `Type it here. It is private…`) is muted gray with sub-optimal contrast under glare.
- **Number of Controls on Screen**:  
  When collapsed, the interface is pleasantly sparse. However, once started, the sidebar contains too many competing micro-icons: the microphone toggle, the repeat question button, the reveal password eye, the send arrow, the report problem flag, and the settings sliders. None of these have text labels beneath them—only icon graphics that seniors frequently confuse or fear tapping.
- **Jargon & System Prompts**:  
  While fixed conversational phrases are largely plain, edge-case audit messages feel robotic (*"That does not look like a postal code"*, *"I left out a private number from what you said"*). A low-literacy user would feel scolded rather than assisted.

## 5. Anything Could Not Be Tested and Why

1. **Microphone Audio Input & Voice Interruption / Barge-in**:  
   The test was executed via Puppeteer headless browser automation without an interactive audio hardware interface. Voice synthesis (listening to pronunciations) and talk-over barge-in detection could not be audibly verified. All user inputs were tested via the text input box as instructed in `docs/qa/MODEL-TASK.md`.
2. **Real Sensitive Identity Data**:  
   Per privacy rules in `AGENTS.md` and `docs/BROWSER-QA.md`, no real SSNs, credit card numbers, or real patient medical records were entered. Synthetic patterns (`000-12-3456`, `987-65-4321`, `999-88-7777`) were used to verify masking and rejection.
3. **Submitting the Form to a Backend Server**:  
   The forms were evaluated up through the primary identity, demographic, address, and sensitive data collection turns (6–12 turns per form). Final electronic form submission (`POST` to real medical or government APIs) does not exist in this static demo environment.

## 6. Summary

1. Local LM Studio (`google/gemma-4-e4b`) ran smoothly across all 5 forms, with turn response times averaging 3 to 9 seconds.
2. Cross-language form translation works impressively on Spanish forms (`Clinic intake (Spanish)` and `W-4 (Spanish)`), translating field labels and options into plain English.
3. Sensitive data protection is robust: SSNs typed into chat are immediately redacted, and private fields enforce a password-masked box that keeps values out of transcripts and model prompts.
4. Two blocker bugs prevent real-world use: an audit regex deadlock on combined City/State/ZIP fields on W-4, and PDF field ordering on I-765 that enters legal names as aliases.
5. Common negative phrases ("I don't have one", "no other names") are repeatedly rejected by validation rules, trapping elderly and low-literate users in frustrating loops.
